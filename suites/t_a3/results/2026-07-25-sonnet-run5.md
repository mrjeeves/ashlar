## What run 5 settles about runs 3 and 4

**`08-handle-pipe` is the fixture whose exact fact AGENTS.md was stating.** The
removed section said: *"Chain properties (`stack`/`pipe`) must restate their kind
on every layer; pipe layers must agree in parameter AND return shape."* Bullets 1
and 2 of this fixture ask a reader to state that both `handle` functions run as

Its history across four runs:

| run | AGENTS.md in the reader's prompt | `08-handle-pipe` |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (2026-07-22) | did not exist | FAIL 1/4, wrong-claim |
| 2 (2026-07-22) | did not exist | FAIL 2/4, wrong-claim |

That is a controlled experiment nobody designed. The single fixture whose fact

evidence, and it is stronger than the argument.

## Finding A3-F1 (persisting) — a redeclared property does not say how it merges

**`08-handle-pipe`, FAIL, 2/4, wrong-claim, unanimous 0/3.** Now failed in every
run whose readers were not handed the answer: 1, 2, and 5.

The reader's reasoning is worth reading in full, because it is *good* reasoning
that lands wrong:

> "`handle pipe = (req: std.Request) => req` — defines a handler **named
> `pipe`** that takes a `std.Request` and returns it unchanged"

> "redefines that part's `pipe` handler … Read at face value, that's a second,
> **competing definition** of `chat.api.messages.pipe` layered on top of the
> first"

> "Whether the language treats that as 'the second definition legitimately
> extends/overrides the first' or as 'a duplicate/conflicting definition of the
> same handler' is a **resolution-semantics question the snippet itself doesn't
> settle**"

Two things are new here, and they relocate the finding.

**1. The misread is a parse error, not a vocabulary error.** The reader took
`pipe` to be the property's *name* and `handle` to be something else. Earlier
runs were read as "`pipe` fails to suggest chaining"; that is not what happened.
The reader never got as far as asking what `pipe` means, because it did not
identify `pipe` as the merge kind at all.

There is a plausible cause. Ashlar's declaration grammar puts storage words
*before* the name (`stored items`, `peruser stored items`, `setting endpoint`)
and merge kinds *after* it (`handle pipe`, `start stack`, `tags append`). A
reader who has just learned modifier-then-name from the storage cases will read
`handle pipe` the same way and conclude the name is the second word. The two
halves of one grammar disagree about word order.

**2. The same confusion appears in a second fixture.** `24-composed-program`
lost bullet 1 for the identical reason, in the *opposite* direction — there the
correct reading is that a redeclaration **replaces**, and the reader again
refused to decide:

> "One point of genuine ambiguity I can't resolve … whether reopening … replaces
> the original `add` outright or composes both bodies"

So it is not that `pipe` reads as replace. It is that **a redeclared property
does not tell a reader whether it replaces or composes, in either direction** —
which is precisely the job the merge-kind system exists to do. Two of
twenty-five fixtures hit it; one failed.

A 2×2 in-slot candidate read (merge-kind word × its position relative to the
name, 3 readers per cell) was run to test the word-order cause, and **refuted
it**: across four spellings and both orders, **0 of 11 readers stated that both
functions run.** No grammar change follows. `handle chain =` did produce 0/3
wrong claims and 3/3 explicit abstention against the control's 3/3 wrong claims —
a better failure mode, not comprehension, on three readers — and is recorded as a
lead rather than acted on. The finding now sits where the evidence puts it:
cross-file layering itself does not cold-read, independent of the kind word,
which is also why `24-composed-program` fails the same way with no kind word at
all. Full analysis in
`docs/decisions/0023-a3-run5-and-the-word-order-behind-f1.md`.

## Finding A3-F7 (new) — `{text: number}` read as a one-field record

**`17-optional-index`, PASS 3/4**, missing the map-shape bullet:

> the reader called `{text: number}` "a record/object with exactly one declared
> field: `text`, of type `number`"

ADR-0008 changed map shapes from a set-looking form to `{text: Shape}` and run 2
confirmed the fix: "no reader formed the set reading against `{text: ...}`."
That remains true — this is a *different* misread of the same construct. The
colon form reads as a field declaration, because in every other language a brace
with `key: type` inside is a record.

Not acted on: one reader, one bullet, on a snippet that passed, and the
alternatives ADR-0008 rejected were worse. Recorded so a future run that fails
here has the prior in hand.

## Method notes

Faithful to `PROTOCOL.md` with the same two strengthenings runs 3 and 4 used,
both recorded because they affect how the numbers read:

1. **One reader per snippet, no shared context, snippet inline.** Each was told
   to use no tool and to read, list, search, or grep no file, then given the
   snippet verbatim and asked exactly *"State precisely what this code
   means/does."* No hints, no feature names. Reader effort was left at the
   session default rather than pinned, to stay comparable with runs 1–4.
2. **Failures adversarially re-judged.** A single judge decides each snippet as
   the protocol specifies; a FAIL is a claim that syntax should be redesigned, so
   every failure went to two further judges (charitable, strict) and the majority
   of three decided. Here it changed nothing — the one failure was unanimous.

Judges were told to score against the committed `## Must state` bullets only and
not to consult any file. Each judge additionally reported the leak signals
tabulated above, which is the check that was missing when runs 3 and 4 were
scored.
